# Pilot playbook

Goal: 2-3 colleges using LanternED weekly by mid-December, each willing to say so in writing and to pay from the next term.

## 1. Choosing pilot colleges

Pick for learning, not for size.

| Look for | Why |
|---|---|
| One internal champion: a HoD or senior teacher who will answer your calls | Pilots die without a person inside |
| 800-3,000 students; at least one branch with a real connectivity problem | Big enough to matter, small enough to support personally |
| A principal who will sign a one-page pilot letter | Without it, nothing converts to a contract |
| Mixed: one rural/semi-urban, one town, ideally one polytechnic | You learn which setting needs which feature |

Avoid colleges that want a custom feature before they start, or whose decision-maker you cannot reach within two weeks.

## 2. The pilot letter (one page)

State: duration (one semester, free), what the college provides (a named coordinator, a class list, permission to use anonymised usage numbers), what you provide (setup, training, WhatsApp support in college hours), what happens at the end (a priced proposal, no obligation), and data handling (link to the privacy notice). Both sides sign.

## 3. Setup, day by day

| Day | You do | They do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Platform admin creates the college; share the **college code** with the coordinator only | Coordinator names a delegated college administrator (a teacher) |
| 1 | Grant that teacher college-admin rights; they create branches and sections | Send you the class list (Excel) |
| 2 | Coordinator imports the **class-list CSV** (Institution page, "Check file" first); teachers listed get approved automatically when they register | Share the registration page and the college code with students |
| 3-4 | Train 3-5 champion teachers (60 minutes, on their phones): create a course, schedule a class, upload a PDF, post an assignment, download the attendance CSV | Teachers try it on their own course |
| 5-7 | Sit in on their first live class; fix anything that goes wrong that day | One live class per champion |

Class-list CSV columns: `email, full_name, role, branch, section` (role `student` or `teacher`; section `all` for a teacher covering the whole branch). Students still sign up themselves with the emailed code, so nobody receives a password from you.

## 4. What to measure

All of it is on the admin console usage table or in the course reports.

| Metric | Healthy by week 4 | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Teachers who used it in 2 of the last 3 weeks | 60% of invited | Classes held, submissions per college |
| Students attending at least one live class | 50% of registered | "Students attending" |
| Assignments submitted online | 30% of students | Submissions |
| Live participant-minutes per active student per month | track it; this drives cost | Live minutes |
| Support requests per teacher per week | falling | Your WhatsApp log |

Also ask every champion each fortnight: "What did you stop doing on WhatsApp/Zoom because of this?" That sentence is your case study.

## 5. Weekly rhythm

- Monday: send each coordinator a three-line usage summary (classes held, students attending, one thing that went well).
- Wednesday: 20-minute call with the champion teachers; collect the top three annoyances.
- Friday: ship at least one fix they asked for and tell them. Visible responsiveness is what converts pilots.

## 6. Converting to paid (weeks 8-10)

1. Share the usage numbers and the champions' quotes with the principal in a 30-minute meeting.
2. Present the priced proposal (see `PRICING_AND_SALES.md`), with the pilot discount valid for 14 days.
3. Offer a first paid term at the pilot-rate with a 60-day opt-out. Remove their fear of lock-in.
4. Ask for a referral to two peer colleges and a recorded testimonial.

If a pilot is not converting, the cause is almost always one of: no internal champion, the principal never saw the numbers, or exams interrupted usage. Fix those before changing the product.
